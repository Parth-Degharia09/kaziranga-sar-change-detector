How to read this & scientific limits

1. Ecological context (Kaziranga 2026): Seasonal inundation is vital for Kaziranga's grasslands and wetlands. On 21 July 2026—about a day and a half before our Jul 22 UTC image—park authorities reported no flood situation in Kaziranga, with only 4 forest camps inundated. This observatory tracks surface-water dynamics rather than claiming a flood disaster.

2. Water-like vs. confirmed flood: Smooth surfaces act like a mirror to L-band radar (< −15.5 dB in HHHH). Permanent river channels, wetlands (beels), flooded paddies, smooth wet mudflats, and dry smooth river sandbars can all share this low-backscatter signature. Switch to the Satellite basemap to inspect exposed sandbars.

3. Experimental vegetation layer caution: Radar backscatter over grass and forest shifts with seasonal plant growth, canopy structure, moisture, and surface-water interactions. These exploratory thresholds indicate possible radar changes, not confirmed vegetation damage.

4. Missing Track 040/Frame 015 interval: No matching NISAR observation is available for this track between 27 July and 10 August 2026, so higher water could have occurred during that interval.
