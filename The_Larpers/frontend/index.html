Methodology note

Radar supplies its own light. The satellite sends a microwave pulse and records the echo. Calm floodwater acts like a mirror: it deflects the pulse away from the sensor (specular reflection), so almost nothing returns and the pixel reads dark, below about −18 dB in VV. Rough ground, crops and buildings scatter energy back and read bright.

Microwaves pass through cloud, so monsoon skies do not interrupt the record the way they do for optical sensors such as Sentinel-2.

Dense canopy is a harder case. Sentinel-1’s C-band (about 5.6 cm wavelength) is largely scattered by leaves. NISAR’s L-band (about 24 cm) reaches the forest floor in places like Kaziranga, and water under vegetation returns a strong double-bounce echo, a bright signature that a simple dark-pixel threshold would miss.
