Radar details & definitions (advanced)

Grid & calibration check: All 20 GeoTIFFs share an identical 3119 × 6977 grid in EPSG:32646 (10 m pixels) calibrated to terrain-corrected linear backscatter power ($\gamma^0$), converted to dB after 50 m spatial speckle averaging.

Vegetation radar change definition: Measured over non-water vegetated land (HVHV > −20 dB in Oct 14) as the disjoint union of patches $\ge 2,500\text{ m}^2$ with either Increased HH ($\Delta\text{HH} > +2.5\text{ dB}$ and $\Delta\text{HH} > \Delta\text{HV} + 1.0\text{ dB}$) or Changed HV ($|\Delta\text{HV}| > 2.5\text{ dB}$).

Why HH over HV for water? Across all 10 dates, HHHH separates smooth water from land by 10.5 dB on average vs. 8.5 dB for HVHV.

How this connects to the radar map
  • Timing fits. The Brahmaputra’s mean monthly flow is highest in July (about 19% of the annual total, Springer), which is consistent with the Jul 22 peak in water-like area seen by NISAR.
  • Radar sees through monsoon cloud. L-band radar sends its own signal, so it can map water when optical satellites see only cloud.
  • Beels show up as dark patches. Assam has more than 3,000 wetlands that hold floodwater (Radiance Weekly); smooth water looks dark to radar.
  • Not every flood is a disaster. Conservationists argue Kaziranga needs its floods, but only in measure.
Facts are compiled from the sources linked above. They are published figures and estimates, not measurements made by this project.
How to read this & scientific limits

1. Ecological context (Kaziranga 2026): Seasonal inundation is vital for Kaziranga's grasslands and wetlands. On 21 July 2026 (one day before our Jul 22 image), park officials reported normal seasonal wetting with only 4 forest camps inundated and no flood emergency—so this observatory tracks surface-water dynamics rather than claiming a flood disaster.

2. Water-like vs. confirmed flood: Calm water acts like a mirror to L-band radar (< −15.5 dB in HHHH). River channels, wetlands (beels), flooded paddies, and smooth wet mudflats all share this dark signature.

3. Vegetation radar change caution: Radar backscatter over grass and forest shifts with structure, moisture, and surface-water interactions. These observations indicate possible radar changes, not confirmed vegetation damage.

4. Missing Track 040/Frame 015 interval: No matching NISAR observation is available for this track between 27 July and 10 August 2026, so higher water could have occurred during that interval.

Future uses
  • Clip directly to official Kaziranga National Park boundary polygons to compute exact inside-park inundation percentages.
  • Extend the reproducible Python pipeline to other dynamic river basins such as Bihar's Koshi floodplain once comparable NISAR tracks are acquired.
  • Pair NISAR 12-day passes with daily NASA GPM IMERG rainfall and Central Water Commission (CWC) river gauge levels.
  • Combine L-band HH/HV double-bounce ratios with elevation (DEM) to map standing water beneath tall grass and forest canopy.
